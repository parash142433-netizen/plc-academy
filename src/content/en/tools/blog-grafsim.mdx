## What GrafSim is

**GrafSim** is a GRAFCET editor and simulator that runs entirely in the browser. It is built on the model the **IEC 60848** standard requires: the transition is an element in its own right, with its preceding and following steps, and the engine applies the **five evolution rules** exactly as they are written.

That is not an academic detail. It is what makes it possible to represent an **AND convergence** that genuinely waits for every branch to arrive, to have two simultaneously fireable transitions fire together, and to have a continuous action drop to zero the moment its step deactivates.

## Drawing with the mouse

Move the mouse near a step and a **connector** appears on its bottom edge. Drag it onto another step and the two get linked: the **transition in between is created for you**, because in GRAFCET two steps are never joined directly. Drop it on empty space and both the transition and the next step are created at once.

With a step selected you also get buttons to continue the sequence, open divergences or close a loop; and **right-clicking** anywhere opens a menu with everything you can do there.

![Guided tutorial inside GrafSim, highlighting an element of the interface](./assets/images/blog-grafsim/grafsim-en-10-tutorial.png)

The tool ships with a 17-step guided tutorial that advances on its own as you do each thing.

## Receptivities and time delays

Receptivities use the notation of the standard and are validated as you type. On top of that, the variables you have declared **are suggested for you**, filtered by what you have typed so far.

*   `a·b`, `a+b`, `!a` and parentheses
*   `↑a` and `↓a` for rising and falling edges
*   `X3` for step activity
*   `t/X3/5s` for a standard time delay
*   `=1` for an always-true receptivity

![GrafSim receptivity field showing the dropdown of declared variables](./assets/images/blog-grafsim/grafsim-en-02-sugerencias.png)

As you type, declared variables are offered grouped by inputs, outputs and steps.

## OR and AND divergences

The bars are not drawn by hand: they are **derived from the topology** of the diagram. If a step has several outgoing links it is an OR divergence and the bar is single; if it is a transition that has several outgoing links, it is an AND divergence and the bar is double. Always consistent with what is actually drawn.

![AND divergence and convergence in GrafSim, with double bar and two simultaneous branches](./assets/images/blog-grafsim/grafsim-en-03-and.png)

## Simulating and watching the signals

While simulating, the active step is painted green with its token, and each transition is coloured by state: **amber** when it is enabled and **green** when its receptivity is also true. That shows you _why_ the GRAFCET advances, or why it does not.

Inputs are toggled **by clicking them**, and you can do it right on the diagram: every variable in a receptivity is a live object. Timers show a progress bar and the remaining seconds, and the timing chart at the bottom plots steps, inputs and outputs.

## Standard diagnostics

GrafSim validates the diagram as you draw and flags what the standard does not allow: no initial step, transitions without a preceding or following step, undeclared variables, unreachable steps or steps that deadlock the cycle, and **OR divergences whose branches are not mutually exclusive** —that last one properly checked, by enumerating the combinations of the variables involved.

![GrafSim diagnostics panel showing the detected structures and the simulation log](./assets/images/blog-grafsim/grafsim-en-07-diagnostico.png)

Diagnostics also lists the detected structures and logs every firing.

## PLC code

Every diagram can be turned into **Siemens SCL**, **CODESYS ST** or plain pseudocode for documentation. It is not a text dump: it follows the two-phase structure a cyclic-scan PLC needs —fireable transitions are computed first, then steps are deactivated and activated— which is precisely what guarantees rules 4 and 5 of the standard.

```iecst
// ─── Phase 1: fireable transitions (rule 2) ───
T1 := X1 AND (start AND part);
T4 := X4 AND X5 AND (TRUE);

// ─── Phase 2a: deactivate preceding steps (rule 3) ───
IF T1 THEN X1 := FALSE; END_IF;

// ─── Phase 2b: activate following steps ───
// This comes after ALL deactivations, so a step that is deactivated
// and activated at once stays active (rule 5).
IF T1 THEN X2 := TRUE; END_IF;

// ─── Actions associated with the steps ───
drill := X2 OR X3;   // continuous action N
```

## Other details

*   **Light and dark themes**, using the site palette.
*   **Save and open** as JSON, and export the diagram as **vector SVG**, ready to print or drop into a document.
*   **It imports diagrams from the old GrafSim**: every connection becomes a transition, and anything without a standard equivalent is reported.
*   **Undo and redo** with Ctrl+Z, plus autosave in the browser.
*   **Built-in examples**: sequence with a loop, sequence selection and simultaneous sequences.
